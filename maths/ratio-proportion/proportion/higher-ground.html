01 / Direct proportion

A constant ratio

y ∝ x → y = kx

Find k = y/x from one pair, write the complete equation, then substitute the new value. Multiplying x by a factor multiplies y by the same factor.

Worked example & common trap

If y = 14 when x = 4, k = 3.5, so y = 3.5x. At x = 8, y = 28. The graph is a straight line through the origin. y = 3.5x + 2 is not direct proportion.

02 / Inverse proportion

A constant product

y ∝ 1/x → y = k/x

Find k = xy. Multiplying x by a factor divides y by that factor. For positive k and x, the graph curves downwards and never reaches either axis.

Worked example & common trap

When x = 4 and y = 9, k = 36. At x = 12, y = 3. Not every decreasing relationship is inverse proportion: test whether xy is constant.

03 / Powers & roots

Translate every word

y = kx²   y = k√x   y = k/x²

“Square of” means x²; “square root of” means √x; “inversely” places that expression in the denominator. Substitute into the exact model to find k.

Scaling shortcut

If x is multiplied by f, y = kx² scales by f²; y = k√x by √f; y = k/x² by 1/f². A 20% increase in x means f = 1.2, not 0.2. For y = kx², y increases by 44%.

04 / Formulae

Substitute, then calculate

E = ½mv²

Use consistent units and brackets for negative values. Follow the order of operations. Here, only v is squared. If m = 4 and v = 3, E = ½ × 4 × 9 = 18.

Accuracy & units

Keep full calculator values until the final rounding step. If a length is converted by a factor of 100, area converts by 100² and volume by 100³. Check whether the question asks for an exact value or a rounded one.

05 / Rearranging

Undo operations in reverse

v = u + at → a = (v − u)/t

Do the same thing to both sides. Clear fractions, isolate powers or roots, and keep brackets around whole numerators. If the subject occurs twice, collect its terms and factorise.

Subject on both sides

ax + b = cx + d → ax − cx = d − b → x(a − c) = d − b → x = (d − b)/(a − c), provided a ≠ c. Substitute your answer back to check.

06 / Grade 9 habits

Connect the ideas

Use different constants for linked models. Divide two equations to eliminate a constant. Read graph axes carefully: a straight line of y against x² suggests y = kx² if it passes through the origin.

A four-line exam routine
  1. State the model or original formula.
  2. Show how the known values determine the constant.
  3. Substitute or rearrange with visible steps.
  4. Check direction, size, units and any positive-root restriction.

For a proof, use algebra for all permitted values, not just numerical examples.
