Quartiles: The Banana Competition Lab 🍌

Understand what Q1, Q2, Q3, IQR, range and outliers mean — not just the formulas.

THE CORE IDEA
Quartiles are gates in an ordered crowd — not averages of four groups.

Imagine 100 banana contestants lined up from the person who ate the fewest bananas to the person who ate the most.

Q1 = 25th-percentile gate
About 25% of observations are at or below this point.
Q2 = 50th-percentile gate
This is the median: the middle of the ordered data.
Q3 = 75th-percentile gate
This is roughly where the highest 25% begins.
“Fourth quartile” ≠ Q4 boundary
The top 25% may be called the fourth quartile, but the usual boundaries are Q1, Q2 and Q3. The far end is the maximum.
Tap each misconception

These are the traps worth learning to reject.

❌ No. Q1 is a boundary value near the 25th percentile. It is not the mean of that group.
❌ No. Q2 is the median of the whole ordered data set.
❌ No. Q3 is the gate near 75%. The highest 25% lies beyond it.
❌ No. IQR is not an average and not 80%. It is the width of the middle 50%: Q3 − Q1.
❌ Definitely not. The top 25% are simply larger observations. An outlier must be much farther away, beyond an outlier fence.
Say it in banana language

If Q1 = 4, Q2 = 7 and Q3 = 11 bananas:

Q1: lower-quarter gate around 4 bananas.
Q2: middle contestant around 7 bananas.
Q3: top-quarter gate around 11 bananas.

IQR = 11 − 4 = 7 bananas means the middle half of the crowd spans a 7-banana-wide interval.

Why this matters for data quality

Quartiles help you notice unusual values without letting those unusual values define the whole story. A suspicious value might be a typing error, a broken sensor, or a genuinely extraordinary contestant.

Good statistical habit: flag an outlier for investigation; do not automatically delete it.

OUTLIER LAB
Build the fences yourself.

Enter Q1, Q3 and a value x. The lab calculates the IQR and both outlier boundaries.

The fence recipe
  1. Find IQR = Q3 − Q1.
  2. Multiply it by 1.5.
  3. Lower fence = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR.
  4. Upper fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR.
  5. A value is an outlier if it is below the lower fence or above the upper fence.
GCSE wording trap: if a value is exactly equal to a fence, it is not beyond the fence. “Below” and “above” are strict.

Think IQR.

Think range.
