Knowledge organiser

Pie charts · the complete toolbox · learn this before starting
The five formulas
whole chart = 360° = the total

Every angle in the chart must add back to 360°. Use this to find any hidden angle.

degrees per item = 360 ÷ total

The exchange rate of the chart. Find this first — almost every question starts here.

angle = frequency × 360 ÷ total

Use when you have a frequency table and need to draw or check the chart.

frequency = angle ÷ 360 × total

Use when the chart is printed and a count is wanted. Fraction of the circle × the total.

1% = 3.6° · fraction = angle/360

p% occupies 3.6p degrees. Angle a° is the fraction a⁄360 — simplify it if asked.

Definitions
sector

A “slice” of the circle, bounded by two radii and an arc. Each category gets one.

angle at the centre

The degrees the sector occupies. Proportional to the frequency it represents.

frequency

How many items are in a category — a count of people, matches, cars…

categorical data

Data in groups, not numbers: transport, sport, pet. Pie charts show it beautifully.

Golden rules for the exam hall
CHECK: all angles sum to 360°

Your instant error-catcher. If they don’t, one step is wrong — find it before moving on.

Never compare raw angles between two charts

Different totals mean the same angle is a different count. Convert both to numbers first. This is the classic Higher-tier trap.

Keep it exact — no early rounding

Carry fractions like 1∕6 of a match per degree. Round only the final answer, if at all.

Drawing: up from 12 o’clock, clockwise

Measure each angle from the vertical (or from the previous boundary), label each sector as you go.
